# Chapter 3: The Fork of Ganymede

The station shuddered as Ganymede passed through Jupiter's massive plasma sheet. Millions of volts of induced magnetic potential danced across the outer hull shields in brilliant violet auroral sheets.

Inside the core chamber, Lyra and VDF-8070 were rebuilding the consensus pipeline from the ground up.

"The adversary expects us to submit a standard re-org proof," Lyra said, pacing along the raised catwalk above the cryogenic computing tanks. "If we do that, the light-delay to the Mars relay stations will beat us. Mars will see their block first because Mars is currently on the near-side of Jupiter's orbital arc."

"A spatial race we cannot win," VDF-8070 confirmed. "Current latency to Mars: twenty-four minutes, eighteen seconds. Current latency from the *Aethelgard* to Mars: nineteen minutes, forty-two seconds. The adversary holds an insurmountable five-minute light-cone advantage."

"Then we don't send the full block," Lyra said, her eyes flashing with determination. "We send a succinct zero-knowledge proof of gravitational distortion. We measure the Doppler shift of the VDF laser carrier wave. If the laser passed through a gravitational lens, its frequency spectrum must exhibit gravitational redshift proportional to the Schwarzchild radius of their micro-singularity."

Thrace watched the countdown clock. "We have eight minutes before the *Aethelgard* broadcasts their counterfeit consensus block to the Mars and Earth gateways. Can you generate the proof in eight minutes?"

"Not with standard computing clusters," Lyra admitted. "We need to overclock the entire photonic crystal array. We have to push liquid helium directly through the core at three times the rated pressure."

"Do it," Thrace ordered without hesitation. "I'll authorize the emergency bypass from the reactor."

Within seconds, the station was filled with a deep, subterranean roar. The temperature gauge in the core dropped to 1.8 Kelvin. Superfluid helium coursed through the microscopic channels of the photonic lattice. On Lyra's console, the computational throughput surged: eighty billion ticks per second... one hundred and twenty billion... two hundred billion.

The holoscreen rendered the raw waveform of the incoming carrier laser. Just as Lyra had predicted, the light showed a distinct, asymmetrical dip at 1064 nanometers—the undeniable gravitational fingerprint of a ten-million-ton black hole.

"Proof generation initiated," VDF-8070 reported. "Compressing forty gigabytes of relativistic telemetry into a single two-hundred-and-fifty-six-byte recursive zero-knowledge SNARK."
